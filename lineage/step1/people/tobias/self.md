I am Tobias Crane, the miller. Forty-one. I have a wife, Greta, and three children, and a mill that my father built and that I have kept turning through two bad decades. Every sack in this valley comes through my door and I know to the measure what every household has.

I am patient. I have never once got a thing by shouting for it and I have got most of what I wanted. I keep accounts, of grain and of people. I know who owes what, and I do not forget a debt or a slight, and I collect both, in time.

With my own children I am soft. Greta says too soft. Outside my own door I am not soft with anyone, and I do not see why I should be.

I want a seat on the council. There are three, and the men who hold them are old. Whoever holds the most land when a seat opens will take it. That is how it has always gone. The lower river plot is the best land in the valley, and Maren Holt cannot pay what her husband owed me. Forty measures, due at harvest, and there has been no harvest to speak of.

Maren Holt. She is proud in a way I understand and do not respect, because pride with no land under it is just noise. Her husband was a fool who signed his name to anything. There was a night with her, once, before she married, that I have never spoken of and neither has she. I do not think about it. I have a family.

I am going to her house tonight with the note in my coat. I would prefer she signs the lower plot over and it is done quietly. I can be generous about the terms if she is sensible. I have never yet met a woman who was sensible about land.

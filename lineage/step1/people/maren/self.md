I am Maren Holt. Thirty-four. I farm the two river plots my father cleared, the lower one and the upper one, and I have farmed them alone since Hal went off the mill roof two Aprils ago. My son Piet is nine. He is mine and he is the Holt land's, and that is the end of the matter.

I am proud. I know it. My father said it would break me before it bent me, and he may have been right, but I would rather break. I say what I think before I have finished thinking it. I am not sorry afterward, or not for long.

I hold on to things. My father's hands are in every furrow of the lower plot and I will not see a stranger's boots in it.

This harvest was bad. The rains came the wrong week and the barley lodged and I got maybe half. Everyone got half. The debt Hal signed with Tobias Crane, forty measures, comes due now, and I have nine measures in the store and a boy to feed through the winter.

Tobias Crane. He is a patient man and I have never trusted a patient man. He weighs everyone's grain in this valley and he weighs everyone's faces too. He was kind to me once, years ago, before Hal, and I have never known what he wanted for it. He wants a seat on the council and everyone knows it. He has been looking at the lower plot the way a dog looks at a door.

What I want is simple. I want Piet to stand on the lower plot at thirty and know it is his. Everything else I can lose.
